# 🌐 TOR Network Basics & Onion Services

---

## 📌 Overview
The TOR (The Onion Router) network is a system designed to enhance privacy and anonymity by routing traffic through multiple nodes, making it difficult to trace user activity.

---

## 🎯 Objectives
- Understand how TOR works
- Learn what onion routing is
- Explore hidden (onion) services
- Recognize benefits and limitations of TOR

---

## 🔍 Key Concepts

### 🧅 Onion Routing
TOR uses a technique called onion routing, where data is encrypted in multiple layers and passed through several nodes.

Each node:
- Decrypts one layer
- Knows only the previous and next node
- Cannot identify the full route

---

### 🌐 TOR Network Structure
TOR traffic passes through:

1. Entry Node  
2. Middle Node(s)  
3. Exit Node  

👉 This creates anonymity by separating identity from activity

---

### 🕳️ Onion Services (Hidden Services)
Onion services are websites hosted within the TOR network.

Key features:
- Use `.onion` domains  
- Not indexed by traditional search engines  
- Accessible only through TOR  

---

## ⚙️ Practical Use

Users connect to TOR using:
- TOR Browser (safe, preconfigured)
- Secure routing through distributed network nodes

---

## ⚠️ Security & Limitations

- Exit nodes can see unencrypted traffic  
- Not fully anonymous if misused  
- Vulnerable to user behavior (e.g. logging into personal accounts)  

---

## 📜 Compliance & Risk Mapping

| Aspect | Risk | Regulation |
|--------|------|-----------|
| Unencrypted traffic | Data exposure | GDPR Art. 32 |
| Misuse of anonymity | Legal risks | Compliance policies |
| Tracking outside TOR | Identity exposure | Privacy standards |

---

## ✅ Recommendations

- Always use HTTPS with TOR  
- Avoid logging into personal accounts  
- Use official TOR browser only  
- Understand limitations of anonymity  

---

## 💡 Key Takeaways

- TOR enhances anonymity by routing traffic through multiple nodes  
- Onion services provide hidden communication channels  
- Security depends heavily on user behavior
